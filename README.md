# CS_330_Artifact

# 1) How do I approach designing software?

# What new design skills has your work on the project helped you to craft?

When designing a 3D scene, I now keep the overall end design and final result in mind. I learned that lighting changes the base colors of objects, so I think about my designs more as a whole instead of focusing on single parts. I also learned to see 2D scenes in a 3D way by thinking in terms of shapes and meshes. This has trained my mind to look at designs in a more advanced and upgraded way, especially when it comes to depth and spatial understanding.

# What design process did you follow for your project work?

I first designed my scene in Photoshop, where I created a snowy environment based on the design choices and style that I personally liked. After that, I built my 3D scene based on that image, using textures and design elements similar to the ones in my original 2D creation. This helped me turn my visual idea into a fully built 3D world.

# How could tactics from your design approach be applied in future work?

Next time I am designing something, I will think about the final result as a whole from the beginning and keep in mind how lighting may affect objects and colors. Playing with 3D shapes during this project has helped me understand depth better, and this experience will help me design future art and projects with more depth and stronger visual impact.

# 2) How do I approach developing programs?

# What new development strategies did you use while working on your 3D scene?

The new development strategies I learned and used during the creation of my 3D scene included working with primitive shapes in OpenGL, applying transformations to them, adding textures, setting materials, and working with lighting. I applied each strategy step by step as it was taught, and then used them in my own way to create the scene I envisioned.

# How did iteration factor into your development?

Iteration played an important role in my development process. I often tested my scene, made small changes, and adjusted things like object placement, lighting, and textures until they looked right. Instead of expecting everything to work perfectly the first time, I improved my project gradually by making continuous refinements and learning from each adjustment.

# How has your approach to developing code evolved throughout the milestones, which led you to the project’s completion?

At the beginning, everything felt more complex, and I sometimes completed steps in an order that caused extra work later. As I progressed through the milestones, I became more organized and structured in my approach. I started completing tasks in a logical, step by step order so that less cleanup work was needed at the end. Over time, my development process became more efficient and thoughtful. After completing each milestone, I found it easier to apply that same technique to the other objects in the scene, showing how my understanding was evolving by applying what I learned to the rest of the scene.
